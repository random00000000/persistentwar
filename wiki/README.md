The blade is still a `Wrong tool` on route doctrine, but a very close rear or blind-side slip can now reliably drop an isolated hostile before the room wakes. Very-close knife contact also no longer fails just because a doorway edge clips bullet-style line of sight, which keeps desperate corner cuts hard but actually registerable. The `knife-extreme` verify stages that exact Broken Signal back-angle pocket so future agents can keep the knife hard but still functional.

That keeps the showcase focused on `equip knife -> enter raid -> land desperate close cut` instead of letting random district bodies pollute the final melee audit.

- `src/game/weapons.ts` and `src/game/scene/RaidScene.ts`: the `Cut-Down Mosin` round now reads faster and harsher on screen than the rest of the junk-gun tier. Doorway routing now fans candidate waypoints slightly left and right of the mouth instead of over-favoring one exact threshold point, and spawned reinforcement waves now start with staggered `navigationRecoveryIndex` values so the whole crash does not choose the same first doorway option before stall recovery kicks in.

- `src/game/simulation.ts`: reinforcement waves now re-resolve their entry points against the player’s current room before they queue and again at the exact spawn tick. If the player or the wave target is inside a structure, the crash packet is forced to stage outside that protected obstacle and out of immediate open line-of-sight, which stops intel, extract, noise, and body-alarm waves from materializing inside the same room the player is holding.
- `src/game/simulation.ts` and `src/main.ts`: enemy bodies now have a Hitman-style alarm escalation instead of being only loot or recovery props. When a live hostile sees a fallen body, one caller locks onto the corpse and spends 10 seconds calling it in; if you kill that caller first, the alarm dies. If the call completes, the game queues a six-man `Body Alarm` wave with a PKM lane owner and flank rifles aimed at the corpse location, and shared pressure reads can now label that as a body alarm instead of a generic sweep.
- `src/game/simulation.ts`, `src/main.ts`, and `scripts/project-cli.mjs`: intel is now a held defense problem instead of an instant secure. Pressing a terminal immediately triggers the `Intel Crash`, starts an 80-second uplink hold on the site, and only banks the packet if the player stays in the terminal pocket through the whole channel. The `intel-alarm` proof slice now verifies the crash fires on press, the wave is aimed back at the held site, and the intel is still unbanked while the long hold is live.
- `src/game/simulation.ts`: normal raids no longer spawn the old player-side `frontlineSupports` lane teams. Only real squadmates on the projectile/casualty path stay on the player's side by default, which cuts the immortal-friendly legacy bodies that were still cluttering the scene and soaking gameplay attention. Authored showcases can still stage explicit support tracks when a slice actually needs them.
- `src/game/simulation.ts`: hostile and friendly combatant navigation now holds onto committed corner-bypass waypoints more intelligently instead of flipping between alternate corners when a straight-line test still grazes the same building edge. The shared stall detector for combatants also now matches the more stable support/incident logic, so slow sliding along walls no longer gets misread as a hard stall every few frames. This specifically targets the visible jitter around building corners during room-clear and threshold fights.
- `src/game/simulation.ts`: doorway-routed room clearing now biases threshold crossing over same-obstacle corner bypass. When a hostile or friendly is outside a defended structure and the real path is through a doorway, threshold and same-side doorway candidates are now strongly preferred, opposite-side doorway points are suppressed until the threshold is actually crossed, and generic corner-bypass candidates are penalized on that traversal. This cuts the heatmap-style corner gravity where building-clearing AI kept orbiting the same exterior corners instead of committing through the door.
- `src/game/simulation.ts`: raid openings now stage a real quiet-ingress phase instead of letting first sightlines become instant rushdown. Fresh raids start with a 32 second cold-entry window, opening spawns are pushed farther off the player insert, and enemy contact only fully burns the lane early when the player gets identified up close or makes enough noise to wake the route. This keeps the first beats closer to the player spec's Hitman/Tarkov-like setup fantasy where you can isolate a sentry, reposition, or bypass a pocket before the war state escalates.
- `src/game/simulation.ts`, `src/main.ts`, and `scripts/project-cli.mjs`: the quiet-ingress opening is now readable in the same live boards the player already uses instead of hiding only in behavior. `Noise Discipline` now upgrades to `QUIET INGRESS` while the cold-entry window is live, `Pressure Posture` can explicitly tell the player to cut one sentry or bypass before the district wakes, snapshots now expose `map.noisePressure`, and `verify --id shed-hide` proves the infiltration slice is selling a real cold-entry window instead of a generic quiet lane.
- `src/game/simulation.ts`: raid enemies are now tuned harder toward defensive building and foothold play. Hot-pocket plans bias more bodies into `hold` and `reserve` postures, indoor/postured defenders stop inheriting `rusher` archetypes by default, and anchored hold/reserve enemies now preserve their building anchor, suppress from safer distance, and only peel out once the player has genuinely turned the fight into a room-clear problem. This keeps the raid AI closer to a readable town-clearing pressure model where the player is punished for bad entries and rewarded for mastering rooms, thresholds, and interior follow-through.
