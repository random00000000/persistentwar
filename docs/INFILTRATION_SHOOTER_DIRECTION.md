Squad & Boys SystemCore FantasyThe game is never “you solo”. It is always “me and the boys” — a tight 3-to-4 man Russian volunteer detachment that feels like a real squad. The boys are NPC squadmates with names, voices, personalities, and permanent consequences. They fight beside you, guard positions when you leave, die in real time, and the war remembers them.Squad Attachment RulesYou always deploy with up to 3 assigned “boys” pulled from your stash pool.
Each boy has a fixed role you can tweak with gear from the stash (rifleman, machine-gunner, grenadier, medic, breacher, drone operator, etc.).
Boys can be ordered in real time:“Cover this sector”
“Push with me”
“Hold this house / trench line”
“Watch that tree line”
“Suppress that window”

When you give an order they execute it with emergent behavior. They will shout at the enemy, throw grenades back, blind-fire from trenches, cook grenades, loot on the move, and call out contacts exactly like you described.

Dialogue & Personality (RimWorld-style Emergent)The boys have short, flavorful voice lines that trigger on context. Examples you wanted:Quiet walk in: “Enjoying the walk, comrades… too quiet if you ask me.”
Contact: “Contact! Blue tape on the roof!” / “They’re shouting something — sounds like they’re pissed!”
Suppressive fire: “Keep their heads down, boys!” / “I’m spraying, dust everywhere!”
Grenade in trench: “Grenade back!” / “Cooking one — fire in the hole!”
After a kill: “His gun is brand new… feels like a crime to leave it.”
Dark humor / boasting: “We are superior humans, they just don’t know it yet.” / “Never surrender, even if it’s 22 of us in this trench.”
Loss: “He’s down… we getting the body or what?” / “I’ll call his wife when we get back to stash.”
In bunker downtime: “Someone make coffee, my stamina is trash.” / “Put the movie on, we got 20 minutes before the next push.”

Dialogue reacts to tape colors:Blue tape = “Regulars, standard.”
Green tape = “Elite — these ones are good.”
Yellow tape = “Foreigners… they don’t miss.”

Squad Death & ConsequencesBoys can die permanently (like RimWorld colonists).
When a boy dies the body stays on the battlefield until you recover it (black bag protocol).
Recovery scenes you wanted:Two adults struggling to carry one heavy bag.
“Someone is groaning… that is enough.”
Suicide op to recover a body that goes sideways.

After recovery you can choose to:Call the family / wife from stash.
Hold a small funeral with the remaining boys.
Replace the dead boy with a new recruit (weaker until leveled by successful raids).

Permanent loss creates real weight: “Me and the boys got killed… pinned, surrounded by Ukrainians — never surrender.”

Squad Persistence When You ExtractYou can leave the boys holding a position (village, trench line, basement strongpoint).
The war continues in real time. They can:Fight and win/lose the position.
Get overrun.
Call for supply drop (you can return later with ammo/food).
Survive 60 days in a position everyone thought was lost.

When you come back the map remembers: new trenches they dug, bodies still there, flag still planted, or the position now flying enemy colors.

Squad as Your Memory LayerThe boys are not disposable bots. They are the emotional core that makes extraction hurt and makes successful raids feel earned.This system turns every raid into a story:“Me and the boys arrived with the logi truck…”
“Me and the boys holding the village…”
“Chillin with the boys in our new country…”
“Tomorrow we push and possibly die.”

NEW SECTION – Persistent Frontline SimulationCore FantasyThe war is alive and never pauses. Even when you extract back to stash, the frontline keeps moving. Villages change hands, trenches get dug or overrun, compounds get reinforced or wiped out, and your boys can fight, die, or hold ground without you.  You come back hours or days later and the map has memory: the logi truck you left is now burning, the Russian flag you planted is still there or gone, the 22-man chosen company you left in the trench is now just black bags and stories.This is what makes the game feel like a real, constant modern frontline war — but 100 % fictional.How the Simulation WorksThe entire map runs a light persistent simulation layer that ticks forward in real time (even when you are offline or extracted).
Every sector (village, tree line, trench network, industrial compound, bunker chain) has a hidden “control state” that updates based on:Squad activity (your boys + AI squads on both sides)
Noise and combat from previous raids
Random frontline events (artillery barrages, armored pushes, supply runs)

When you return, the map shows the results of what happened while you were gone.

Key Situations You Wanted (Fully Preserved)You and the boys arrive at the location with the logi truck. The boys immediately start digging a trench (skill-based mini-game).  
You set the boys to guard a village or trench line and extract. When you come back they may have:Held the position and dug deeper trenches.
Been reinforced by more Russians emerging from buildings.
Gotten pinned and surrounded by Ukrainians (“never surrender”).
Been completely wiped — 22 Russians in a trench, chosen company gone.

The war plays out even if you do nothing:A quiet sector suddenly explodes when an armored Ukrainian brigade rolls in.
Artillery falls on your old mortar position — NPCs scatter, one car is smoking.
A caravan of 16 Russian armored trucks drives straight into the most obvious trap — all vehicles lost, tank kill zone, diverse explosions (big ammo cook-off, little sparks, burning tracked vehicle, smoke columns).
A white van arrives at a compound, Russians emerge, then the logi truck turns out to be carrying anti-air or comms gear and gets ambushed.

Positions evolve visibly:New custom trenches and bunkers appear where your boys or friendly squads fought.
Destroyed tanks and armored cars stay on the map with realistic damage (smoke, sparks, different explosion types).
Mortar positions get hit — craters, scattered gear, groaning wounded.

Long-term persistence stories:A lone survivor holds a bunker for 60 days — everyone thought he was dead.
You return to recover bodies that are still exactly where they fell.
Sympathizer civilians are still hiding in the same house, refusing to evacuate.
The frontline shifts: a village you cleared is now back under Blue control with new Ukrainian tape colors moving in.

Connection to Extraction & SquadThe simulation makes every extraction decision heavier:
“If I leave now, will my boys still be alive when I come back with more grenades?”
“If I stay and greed one more compound, will the whole sector go hot and my extract route collapse?”
When you drop supplies or reinforce a position, the simulation reacts — your boys get better gear, dig stronger trenches, or call for help later.
Failed positions create new stories: suicide ops to recover bodies, calling the wife from stash, dark humor in the barracks (“Tomorrow we push and possibly die”).

Technical Light TouchThe simulation is not full real-time strategy. It uses smart event tables and squad-level AI so it stays performant.
Player sees the results through:Visual map changes (new trenches, destroyed vehicles, flags, body bags).
Radio chatter and boy dialogue when you return.
Dynamic mission opportunities (“Your boys are pinned — need resupply now” or “Position held for 60 days — they’re legends”).

This system turns the game into a living war instead of a series of disconnected raids. Every time you drop back in, the frontline has moved — and “me and the boys” are right in the middle of it.

NEW SECTION – Map Scale & Persistent World StructureCore FantasyThe map must feel and behave like a single Foxhole-sized frontline battlefield — vast, persistent, and alive — but built for a tight 3–4 man Russian squad extraction experience.
You are not playing the entire war. You are one small detachment operating inside one massive, constantly shifting “Dead Zone” that feels as big and consequential as a full Foxhole region.Locked Map ScaleOverall size: One continuous playable map the same scale as a single Foxhole region (approximately 8–12 km² in real-world equivalent, but compressed intelligently for gameplay flow).  Foot travel across the full map should take 45–90 minutes one way.  
Vehicle/logi truck travel should feel meaningful (10–25 minutes between major battlefield zones).

This replaces the current small “tech scene” prototype map. The new map is the full persistent Dead Zone from day one.

Map Structure & LayersThe map is divided into dynamic battlefield zones that the persistent simulation constantly updates. Each battlefield zone can be:Quiet (lull period)
Hot (active fighting)
Controlled (Russian-held with your boys or friendly squads)
Contested / Lost (Blue forces dominant)

Battlefield Zone Types (directly from your verbs):Shattered villages (house-to-house clearing, basements as strongpoints, civilians hiding)
Tree-line fighting (ambushes, creeping advances)
Urban warfare blocks (ruined apartment buildings, blind fire from windows)
Trench networks & bunker chains (custom diggable trenches, firing ports, unpenetrable positions in late game)
Industrial compounds & long-abandoned factories (white vans, logi trucks, ambushes)
Shell-crater fields and open ground between battlefield zones

Battlefield zones are connected by natural routes:Tree lines and roads
Civilian car paths
Logi truck routes
Hidden infiltration paths

Persistent Simulation Across the Whole MapThe entire map runs the Persistent Frontline Simulation at all times.
When you extract, the simulation keeps ticking across all battlefield zones:Your boys can be left holding one village while a 16-truck Russian armored caravan gets wiped in another battlefield zone 4 km away.
Artillery can hit a mortar position you visited yesterday.
A new Ukrainian armored brigade can roll into a previously quiet battlefield zone and change the entire front.

You can insert anywhere on the map via logi truck or foot from stash, but longer travel = higher risk and more time for the war to evolve while you’re moving.

Travel & Scale Tension (Extraction Impact)Travel is part of the raid loop — not instant fast-travel.
Early raid: short insertion to a nearby battlefield zone (low risk, small gains).
Mid/late raid: deeper pushes into hotter battlefield zones (higher greed temptation, escalating danger).
Convoy movement in random civilian cars or armored trucks creates the exact moments you wanted:“Me and the boys rolling in a convoy of the most random civilian cars”
“Caravan of 16 Russian armored trucks walks into the most obvious trap”

Vehicles matter: broken cars, stolen civilian vehicles, logi trucks that can be ambushed or used for supply drops.

Visual & Atmospheric ScaleThe map never feels like a small arena. Even in quiet moments you see distant smoke columns, hear far-off artillery, or spot drone lights on the horizon.
