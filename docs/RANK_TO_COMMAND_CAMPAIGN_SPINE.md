# Rank To Command Campaign Spine

This is the product spine for the first shippable campaign.

The player should not start as a commander with soldiers.

The player starts alone.

They earn command.

## Core Idea

The first five minutes are a top-down shooter.

The player enters the war personally, fights, loots, survives, and levels up.

After ranking up, the player is trusted with a small camp and one soldier.

From there, the game grows into the officer fantasy:

- recruit soldiers
- upgrade camp
- haul supplies
- build trenches
- hold ground
- create logistics
- assault the final enemy camp

The player's rank is the bridge between lone shooter and war commander.

## Player Journey

## Phase 1: Alone In The War

The player starts with no soldiers.

They have:

- a basic weapon
- limited ammo
- no camp command
- no recruitable units
- no trench authority
- no logistics layer

The player goal is simple:

Survive, loot supplies, and prove value.

This phase should last around five minutes in the first session.

The player learns:

- movement
- shooting
- looting
- extraction or banking
- danger
- why soldiers would matter

This keeps the game immediately playable before the colony layer opens.

## Phase 2: First Rank

The player ranks up.

This is the first major transition.

The game says, in effect:

"You proved yourself. You are now trusted with a camp responsibility."

Unlocks:

- first camp
- one assigned soldier
- basic camp upgrade menu
- basic recruit path
- simple build order
- first trench order

The first soldier should feel important.

They are not a generic unit. They are the first person assigned to the player.

## Phase 3: First Camp

The player now has a small camp.

The camp starts weak.

It can support only a small number of people.

The player must decide whether to:

- haul supplies back to camp
- build at the contested supply area
- recruit another soldier
- improve the camp
- personally run dangerous supply trips
- send the first soldier to build or defend

This is where the RimWorld-like layer begins.

## Phase 4: Contested Supplies

Camp upgrades require supplies from contested places.

The player should not passively generate everything at home.

Supplies exist in dangerous locations:

- warehouses
- farm stores
- abandoned checkpoints
- wrecked convoys
- industrial yards
- medical caches
- ammo dumps

The player has two main choices:

### Haul Supplies Home

The player personally or with soldiers brings supplies back to the camp.

Feeling:

"I am feeding the camp by surviving runs."

### Build A Camp Near Supplies

The player establishes a forward camp or outpost near the contested supply area.

Feeling:

"I am moving the war machine forward."

This is the key strategic tension.

Do I carry resources back, or do I build closer to danger?

## Phase 5: Rank Unlocks Soldiers

When the player ranks up, they are assigned more responsibility.

Rank should unlock:

- more soldier capacity
- better recruit quality
- new soldier roles
- more camp authority
- stronger build orders
- larger operations

This means getting more soldiers is not just an economy button.

It is a career progression.

The player earns command over people.

## Example Rank Ladder

### Rank 0: Survivor

No soldiers.

No command.

Goal:

Survive and bring supplies home.

### Rank 1: Acting Corporal

Unlocks:

- first camp
- one assigned soldier
- basic camp storage
- first trench order

Goal:

Keep one soldier alive and make the camp useful.

### Rank 2: Section Lead

Unlocks:

- second and third soldier
- bunks
- med tent
- ammo store
- camp work priorities

Goal:

Create a small working camp and survive enemy pressure.

### Rank 3: Platoon Lead

Unlocks:

- more soldier capacity
- suppressor or defender role
- forward camp
- connected trenches
- logistics assignment

Goal:

Hold a forward position and turn it into a launch point.

### Rank 4: Company Officer

Unlocks:

- larger squad pool
- MG bay
- better camp upgrades
- multi-trench fallback doctrine
- final assault planning

Goal:

Prepare the assault on the enemy camp.

### Rank 5: Assault Commander

Unlocks:

- final camp assault
- siege camp or assault staging
- full commitment of soldiers and stockpile

Goal:

End the war.

## Camp Upgrade Economy

Camp upgrades should cost supplies from contested places.

Use chunky supply types:

- build material
- ammo
- food
- medical stock
- weapons

Avoid factory chains.

The player should not be optimizing production ratios.

The player should be asking:

- Can my camp feed more soldiers?
- Can wounded soldiers recover?
- Can the trench line stay supplied?
- Can I support another recruit?
- Can I afford a forward camp?
- Can I stage the final assault?

## Camp Upgrade Examples

### Storage

Lets the camp hold more supplies.

### Bunks

Raises soldier capacity.

### Med Tent

Returns wounded soldiers to duty.

### Mess

Improves recovery and morale.

### Ammo Store

Supports more defenders and trench resupply.

### Radio Post

Unlocks better command and longer range orders.

### Motor Pool

Improves hauling and forward camp support.

### Command Tent

Unlocks higher rank operations and final assault planning.

## Recruiting Soldiers

Recruitment should be gated by both rank and camp support.

To recruit a soldier, the player needs:

- enough rank
- enough camp capacity
- enough food or shelter
- enough weapons or basic equipment
- enough morale or safety

Recruit sources:

- assigned by command after rank-up
- local volunteers
- rescued stragglers
- survivors from collapsed positions
- reinforcement convoys

The cleanest first version:

Rank-up grants the first soldier.

Later recruits require camp capacity plus supplies.

## The Main Campaign Loop

1. Player fights alone.
2. Player loots supplies.
3. Player ranks up.
4. Player gets first camp and first soldier.
5. Player upgrades camp with contested supplies.
6. Camp supports more soldiers.
7. More soldiers build trenches and defend supply routes.
8. Player builds forward camp near better supplies.
9. Forward camp supports a stronger trench network.
10. Player prepares final assault.
11. Player destroys enemy camp and ends the war.

## Why This Works

The shooter layer gives immediate action.

The rank layer gives progression.

The camp layer gives a home.

The soldier economy gives emotional stakes.

The contested supplies give reason to leave camp.

The forward camp gives strategic expansion.

The final assault gives an end.

## First Shippable Version

Build this order:

### Milestone 1: Solo Start

Player starts with no squadmates and no command layer.

They can fight, loot, and bank supplies.

### Milestone 2: Rank One Unlock

After enough progress, player ranks up and unlocks:

- first camp
- one assigned soldier
- basic camp panel

### Milestone 3: Camp Upgrades

Player spends contested supplies to build:

- storage
- bunks
- med tent
- ammo store

### Milestone 4: Recruit Growth

Camp capacity plus rank allows more soldiers.

Soldiers can be assigned to build, defend, rest, or resupply.

### Milestone 5: Forward Camp

Player can build a simple forward camp near a contested supply area.

It becomes a new staging point if defended.

### Milestone 6: Final Assault

Once the forward camp and army are strong enough, player can launch the final enemy camp assault.

## Key Design Decision

When the player ranks up, should new soldiers be:

### A: Assigned By Command

The game grants soldiers at rank milestones.

This feels military and controlled.

### B: Recruited From Camp

The player chooses recruits if the camp can support them.

This feels more RimWorld-like.

### C: Both

Rank gives assigned soldiers, and camp growth lets the player recruit extras.

Recommendation:

Choose C.

Rank should grant the first soldier so progression is clear.

Camp growth should unlock more recruits so the colony loop matters.
