## Purpose

Lock the next product direction for the project so future work builds a complete tactical squad extraction shooter slice instead of a combat sandbox with disconnected systems.

This document is the north star for the next serious product phase.

It should be read together with the earlier atmosphere and world-intent documents, not as a replacement for all of their feeling.

## Product Statement

`Top Down Extraction Shooter` should become a mastery-driven tactical squad extraction game where the player commands a small team through readable modern combat problems, survives intense operations, extracts alive with meaningful gains, and grows a stash that changes the next run.

The game is not only about good controls.

It is about turning those controls into a full product loop:

- command the boys
- solve tactical spaces
- manage guns with real doctrine
- survive adaptive enemy pressure
- extract with loot, bodies, and squad consequences
- return to a stash that matters

The product should still feel like a militia-style extraction game in a living exclusion zone along a fictional collapsing frontline.

Quiet walks through villages, tree lines, trench systems, bunkers, and ruined apartments should be able to turn into sudden violence at any moment.

Local extraction is survival.

The larger emotional truth is that the squad is trying to survive and push through a war that cannot really be escaped one raid at a time.

You extract from the operation, but the only final way out is to help finish the fictional war itself.

The intended long-form wrapper for that promise is defined in [Endgame Direction](C:/Users/Javier/Desktop/CodexCLI/2d-game-engine/projects/topdownextractionshooter/docs/ENDGAME_DIRECTION.md): the game should be beatable, and the full campaign should ultimately build toward a true escape by surviving and winning a final offensive against the hardest fortified district in the war sector.

## The Main Pivot

The project already has one of its strongest differentiators in place:

- `me and the boys`
- direct command feel
- emergent RimWorld-inspired dialogue

That foundation should stay.

The pivot is that the game now needs the rest of the product to rise to that standard.

The next north star is not `add more random war flavor`.

It is:

`Build a complete tactical squad extraction slice where controller depth, map depth, AI depth, gun identity, stash consequence, and extraction pressure all reinforce one another.`

The cleaner product direction should not strip out the older meaning of the game.

It should preserve:

- `me and the boys`
- the sense of a war that keeps moving without you
- the emotional weight of body recovery, loss, resupply, and return
- the feeling of moving through a living gray zone where towns, compounds, trenches, and roads do not stay solved forever
- the deeper feeling that extraction is temporary relief inside a longer war that still has to be endured and eventually finished

That means the north star is not only about making the loop deep and replayable.

It is also about making the loop point somewhere:

- the player must be able to prepare for a final campaign-ending push
- the stash, squad, map, AI, guns, and extraction systems must all scale into that ending
- the game should eventually let the player truly get out

## Core Player Promise

The player should feel like a small-unit leader entering a dangerous operation with a real tactical toolbox.

Each run should ask the player to:

- read the terrain
- position the squad correctly
- use suppression, movement, crossing, breach, and room-control verbs well
- choose the right gun roles for the space
- protect the squad and the haul
- decide when the operation has become too expensive to continue

The ideal player sentence is:

`We crossed under PKM cover, pinned the defenders in the trench, cleared the building clean, took one casualty, looted what mattered, and got out before the route collapsed.`

Another valid player sentence should be:

`We came back to the same town and it had changed hands again, the flag was gone, there were new bodies in the lane, and the fight demanded a completely different plan.`

The deeper campaign sentence should be:

`We got out alive today, but the war is still here tomorrow.`

## What The Product Is

The game should be treated as:

`A tactical squad extraction shooter centered on a deep command language, masterable AI pressure, granular combat spaces, meaningful guns, stash consequence, and replayable operations.`

It should not be treated as:

- a lone-operator extraction shooter with cosmetic squadmates
- a pure large-scale war simulator
- a stealth game
- an arcade swarm shooter
- a content treadmill that hides shallow systems behind more missions

## Current Strengths To Protect

These are already strong and should be preserved:

- deep-feeling top-down controller
- direct squad command fantasy
- named boys with role identity
- emergent dialogue with memory and personality
- readable top-down combat presentation
- extraction as a promising loop anchor

The dialogue should remain a major part of the product identity, but as flavor and emotional memory rather than the primary system carrying depth by itself.

The right framing is:

`AI tactics and map design create the mastery. Dialogue makes those moments memorable.`

## Target Feeling

The target feeling should still carry the earlier vision's specific texture:

- you and the boys rolling in through a dead sector in beat-up civilian cars or a logi truck
- a quiet approach through a village, trench line, or apartment block
- stretches of slowness where the player has time to think, listen, plan, and feel the weight of the situation
- dust kicking up from gunfire on dirt, concrete, and bunker walls
- suppressive fire forcing crouches, blind fire, hesitation, and pinned movement
- grenades deciding trench and bunker fights
- the choice to loot a fresh enemy gun even when it is tactically risky
- the pressure to recover a body, hold a sector, or pull out before the route gets hotter
- small moments of life between fights like coffee in a trench, bunker downtime, or dark humor from the boys

That atmosphere is not optional decoration.

It is the meaning layer that gives the tactical product emotional identity.

The war should not feel like a nonstop Hollywood set piece.

It should have room for:

- dread before contact
- quiet movement
- bunker and barracks downtime
- slower operational thinking
- the feeling that people are living inside the war, not only performing action scenes inside it

## Product Pillars

### 1. Commandable Depth

The player must have expressive, reliable control over a small squad in real time.

This includes:

- hold
- follow
- attack
- sector watch
- covering movement
- suppression
- grenade delegation
- positional control around buildings, crossings, and trench segments

The player should feel like a field leader with a stable tactical language, not a shooter with follower buffs.

### 2. Tactical Spaces

The map must be made of granular combat problems, not only broad war scenery.

Key combat spaces include:

- doorways
- windows
- alleys
- roads and exposed crossings
- rooms with layered angles
- trench segments and trench junctions
- courtyards
- yard-to-building transitions
- basement and bunker footholds
- extract lanes under pressure

These spaces should appear inside a broader living battlefield:

- shattered towns
- tree lines
- trench networks
- bunker chains
- industrial compounds
- shell-crater fields
- basements used as strongpoints

Every one of these spaces should reward correct tactics and punish lazy entry.

Assaulting a trench, crossing a lane, or clearing a room should not be trivial.

They should feel like real tactical problems the player can practice and improve at.

### 3. Masterable AI Pressure

Enemy AI must operate inside the same tactical language the player is learning.

The AI should be able to:

- pin through suppression
- hold windows, rooms, and trench segments with intent
- punish reckless pushes
- react strongly to casualties
- collapse or surrender when pressure becomes overwhelming
- call for reinforcement, medevac, or fallback behavior when the fight turns
- create situations where one loss changes the tempo of the whole operation
- contest, lose, retake, and reinforce settlements and sectors over time

The goal is not random chaos.

The goal is readable pressure that supports mastery.

The player should lose because they chose poor doctrine or executed badly, not because the battlefield became unreadable noise.

The intended pressure profile should include verbs from the earlier raw fantasy:

- pinned by machine-gun fire
- room defenders holding until they surrender or die
- trench defenders dominating until properly broken
- armored evac or casualty-pull responses altering the shape of the fight
- civilians or noncombatants complicating a push
- enemies shouting, posturing, falling back, or collapsing under pressure

### 4. Guns As Doctrine

Weapons must define tactical solutions, not just stat upgrades.

The next slice needs a clearly established weapon ladder that gives the player and squad distinct battlefield roles:

- `Handguns` for backup survival and last-ditch fighting
- `SMGs` for tight interiors and aggressive entry
- `Shotguns` for room dominance and short-range shock
- `Core rifles` for general-purpose raid work
- `PKM` for suppression, lane denial, and assault support
- `One endgame-changing weapon class` that materially changes tactics and late-game planning

Each gun family should change how the squad solves space, how the AI responds, and how the player thinks about risk.

### 5. Stash Consequence

The stash must stop feeling like a detached inventory shell and become the real operational backbone of the game.

The north star slice should normalize the stash so:

- all meaningful stash items can enter raids in a coherent way
- squad loadouts matter before deployment
- loss and survival change future options
- boys can be replenished in a believable systemized way after casualties
- gear, ammo, meds, and weapon roles tie directly into raid planning

The stash is where the player prepares doctrine, absorbs losses, and decides what the next operation can be.

### 6. Risked Extraction

Extraction is what validates the whole tactical loop.

The player should always feel tension around:

- staying for more loot
- pressing one more objective
- keeping the squad alive
- hauling a body out or abandoning it
- choosing whether the operation is already a win

The run should end because the player makes a hard decision under pressure, not because the map was fully consumed.

But the larger fiction should always remind the player that extraction is not peace.

It is a temporary escape from one operation inside an unfinished war.

### 7. Replayable Operations

The game needs replayability built from tactical variation, not only larger content count.

That means each operation should remix:

- entry angle
- enemy posture
- room pressure
- trench pressure
- extract pressure
- loot incentives
- squad survival state
- weapon fit for the route

Replayability should also come from territorial instability.

The game should behave like a gray zone:

- towns can change hands multiple times
- compounds can be Russian-held, Blue-held, contested, abandoned, or scarred by prior fighting
- a route that felt safe yesterday can be hot today
- a trench line can deepen, collapse, or be retaken
- a village clear is never automatically permanent
- the player returns to places that remember previous fights but do not freeze in that state forever

Players should be able to say:

- `this run I leaned on suppression`
- `this run I cleared buildings properly`
- `this run I used the boys better`
- `this run I got greedy and should have left`
- `we took the town yesterday and had to fight for it again today`

## Shared Tactical Language

This is one of the most important design rules in the project.

Player, boys, and enemies should all be participating in the same battlefield logic.

That logic should revolve around:

- suppression
- angle control
- sector watch
- movement under cover
- crossing danger lanes
- room entry
- trench assault
- casualty response
- collapse, surrender, or withdrawal
- hold, lose, and retake ground

This is how the game becomes chess-like.

Not by becoming turn-based, but by becoming legible, positional, and masterable.

The player should gradually learn:

- when pressure is enough to pin a defender
- when a crossing is too expensive
- when a room is soft enough to push
- when a trench needs grenades before entry
- when a casualty should stop the whole plan
- when extracting now is better than winning one more fight
- when a town is worth holding and when it is smarter to let it go and return later

## Raw Verbs To Preserve

The north star should explicitly protect the raw fantasy verbs that gave the project its original energy.

These are not all day-one scope items, but they are part of the intended identity:

- massive suppressive fire that changes behavior even on near misses
- room clearing, bunker clearing, and trench assault as non-trivial skills
- cooking grenades and throwing grenades back
- blind fire from trenches, windows, and hard cover
- dust and material response from shooting
- pushing with the boys, falling back with the boys, and setting sectors for them to hold
- looting enemy guns that feel too valuable to leave behind
- hiding in sheds, basements, bunkers, and dead spaces while the enemy sweeps nearby
- body recovery, black-bag handling, and the possibility that some bodies cannot be recovered
- convoy arrivals, civilian-car movement, white vans, logi trucks, and battlefield transport as part of the war texture
- armored drops, artillery strikes, mortar hits, and sudden scale changes in the operation
- planting flags, holding villages, losing villages, and retaking villages
- bunker downtime, trench coffee, dark humor, and barracks talk that make the squad feel human

When choosing features, the rule is not `add raw flavor text`.

The rule is:

`translate these verbs into playable, readable, repeatable tactical situations.`

## The Role Of Dialogue

The RimWorld-inspired dialogue layer should remain part of the game's identity.

It is not the primary source of tactical depth, but it is a major source of:

- emotional attachment
- situational flavor
- memory
- humor
- squad personality
- after-action storytelling

The dialogue should support the product by reacting to the tactical game the player is actually playing:

- suppression moments
- room-clear fear and confidence
- trench assault pressure
- casualty and medevac urgency
- loot temptation
- extraction relief
- stash-side grief, pride, and replacement
- sector capture, sector loss, and towns changing hands again

It should also preserve the older fantasy that the boys are people living through a campaign, not sterile tactic announcers:

- joking in bunkers
- dark humor after losses
- boasting, fear, and argument under pressure
- remembered bodies and remembered towns
- small human beats between operations
- the knowledge that surviving one raid does not mean they are truly out

The target is:

`serious tactical depth with human flavor`

not:

`system barks explaining the fight`

## The Next Playable Product Slice

The next major product milestone should be a fully playable tactical squad extraction slice.

It should include:

- one strong replayable main map
- optionally one smaller companion map for tighter tactical drills or alternate pacing
- a functional stash-to-raid-to-extract-to-stash loop
- normalized stash item flow into raids
- replenish-or-replace squad flow after losses
- weapon classes with clearly different doctrinal roles
- tactical map geometry that supports room clears, trench assaults, windows, crossings, and extractions
- AI behaviors that make those spaces worth mastering
- a live territorial layer where at least one town or settlement can change hands repeatedly across operations

The goal is not broad content coverage.

The goal is one strong slice that proves the product.

## Map Direction

The main map should be replayable because it contains many granular tactical situations, not because it is simply large.

The map should support:

- trench fights that require setup and intent
- building clears with meaningful windows, thresholds, and internal angles
- exposed crossings where suppression matters
- layered footholds where the boys can hold while the player advances or falls back
- extract routes that become dangerous in different ways depending on raid state
- towns and compounds that can flip control multiple times over the campaign

The key map fantasy is not just `large warzone`.

It is:

`a living gray zone of towns, trenches, roads, bunkers, and compounds that keep changing state and therefore keep producing new tactical problems.`

If a second map exists, it should not be a filler map.

It should either:

- compress the same tactical language into a smaller high-pressure operation

or:

- teach and stress a different subset of the same doctrine

## AI Direction

The AI must become deep enough that players can study it, exploit it, and improve against it over time.

Priority behaviors for the north star slice:

- effective suppression and pinning
- strong posture in rooms, windows, and trench lines
- meaningful reaction to casualties
- pressure-oriented reinforcement behavior
- medevac or casualty-pull response that changes the fight shape
- collapse and surrender logic when isolated and overwhelmed
- enough variation to create replayable operations without losing readability
- behavior that supports territorial retakes, sector defense, and settlements changing hands

This is single-player-first work.

Single-player should prove the tactical language so later multiplayer has a solid foundation instead of trying to rescue shallow systems.

## Guns Direction

Guns must rise to the occasion of the controller and tactical spaces.

The north star slice should establish that gun choice changes doctrine:

- handguns keep a compromised operator alive
- SMGs and shotguns transform room-entry and interior aggression
- rifles carry the general raid
- PKM changes what suppression and lane control feel like
- the endgame weapon changes late-run planning and threat expectations

Weapons should also help express some of the raw battlefield fantasy:

- PKM-class pressure pinning a crossing or trench lip
- shotguns making interiors terrifying at close range
- SMGs supporting aggressive building entry
- rifles anchoring the broader town or road fight
- found enemy weapons creating greedy mid-raid decisions

This must be felt both in the player's hands and in squad composition.

`Bring the right weapon for the operation` should become a real decision.

## Stash Direction

The stash should become a serious layer of the same product, not a separate management mode.

The north star slice should lock these rules:

- the stash directly feeds raid loadout planning
- squad gear assignment is meaningful
- loot returned from raid becomes operational capability
- deaths, missing bodies, and depletion change what the player can confidently attempt next
- replenishment exists, but replacement should not be emotionally empty

The stash should answer:

- what did I bring
- what did I risk
- what did I gain
- what did I lose
- what does that mean for my next operation

It should also answer:

- can I afford to re-enter that town now
- do I have the boys, ammo, grenades, and guns for a trench or building fight
- am I returning to hold, loot, recover, reinforce, or retake

## Endgame Direction

The north star doc must acknowledge that the extraction slice is not the final product.

It should define an eventual endgame target so the next milestone is building toward something.

The endgame should be framed as:

`A mature tactical squad extraction game where a developed stash, veteran or replacement boys, specialized loadouts, and deep operation knowledge let the player take on high-pressure operations that feel earned rather than merely unlocked.`

The emotional wrapper around that endgame is:

`Each successful extraction keeps the squad alive for now, but the real long-term way out is to survive enough, win enough ground, and push far enough that the fictional war itself can finally end.`

The endgame should eventually include:

- higher-stakes operations that demand real doctrine
- advanced gun and squad role combinations
- heavier consequence for losing a strong team
- more dangerous and more rewarding tactical problems
- a mastery curve where experienced players can feel themselves playing substantially better than they did at the start
- a campaign-level sense that the squad is not only surviving the war, but helping bring it to its end

The endgame should not depend on multiplayer existing first.

## What The Game Must Avoid

Avoid drifting into:

- bigger but tactically emptier maps
- AI difficulty that is only more health or aim
- more guns that do not change tactics
- stash complexity that does not affect raid decisions
- dialogue that explains instead of enriches
- theatrical war flavor without actual tactical depth
- feature accumulation that hides the fact that the core operation is still shallow
- a map-clear mentality where settlements never become meaningful again after one successful run

## Success Criteria

This direction is working when players naturally say things like:

- `I had to suppress that window before we could cross`
- `I rushed the trench and got punished`
- `The PKM changed the whole push`
- `I cleared the first room right and the second room wrong`
- `One casualty changed the whole operation`
- `I should have extracted with what we had`
- `That loadout was wrong for this route`
- `The boys saved the run`
- `I lost a good operator and felt it in the next raid`
- `We cleared that town before, but today it was a different fight`
- `It felt like a crime to leave that gun behind`
- `We made it out, but none of us felt like we were really out`

## Durable Rule

The project should now evaluate future features with this question:

`Does this make the tactical squad extraction slice deeper, more masterable, more replayable, and more consequential?`

If the answer is no, it is probably not north-star work.
